# Analogues: measured benefits of digital/automated procurement programmes

Ticket: [#6](https://github.com/JoranBergfeld/procurement-automation/issues/6) · Map: [#1](https://github.com/JoranBergfeld/procurement-automation/issues/1) · Researched: 2026-11-13

## Question

What measured outcomes (cycle time, cost savings, participation, disputes, programme cost) have comparable programmes reported? Which effect sizes can the value model credibly borrow, and what do such programmes typically cost to build and run?

## Answer (short)

1. **Cycle time is the strongest-evidenced gain, and it is the right one to lead with.** The closest analogue to "agents on standardised below-threshold buys" is Ukraine's **Prozorro Market** e-catalogue, compared with open tenders *on the same digital platform*. It reports **9 days vs ~27 days (about −67%)** across all buyers and **8 vs 19 days (−58%)** for medical goods. The case study's **−60% target is plausible only for standardised, catalogue-like purchases**. Across all below-threshold procedures a **base of about −45%** is more defensible.
2. **Price savings from digitisation alone are small and contested.** The best causal study (India/Indonesia, AEJ 2016) finds **no price effect**. Ukraine above-threshold shows **3.5–5.8%**. A World Bank systematic review puts well-implemented reforms at **5–10%**. Walmart's agent negotiation of tail spend delivered **1.5% (pilot) to 3% (scaled)**. The large figures (Consip "up to 47%", the EC's "5–20%", Prozorro's "UAH 301bn saved") come from aggregation effects or self-reported savings against estimates. **Do not borrow them.** For agent negotiation on below-threshold spend, use **0.5% / 1.5% / 3%**.
3. **No credible measured analogue exists for disputes or for SME participation caused by automation.** Use these as assumption-driven scenarios, and leave the measurement to the pilot. The only "−47% protests" figure found (attributed to a "GSA Digital Acquisition Pilot") **could not be verified on any GSA source and must not be used**.
4. **Platform build costs are modest, and running costs are ongoing.** Builds cost USD 1–9m (Prozorro about USD 5m; Estonia's 2018 register was planned at EUR 2.5m; Korean-built e-GP exports cost USD 1.1–9m). A full national re-platform like next-generation KONEPS cost about EUR 70m. Running costs are **about 15–20% of build cost per year** (KONEPS O&M). The agent system is an *increment on an existing e-procurement stack*, not a new e-GP.

## Recommended effect sizes to borrow (for #9 business-case sheet)

All of these are **assumptions informed by analogues**, not measurements of the Estonian system. Each row states its basis and flags what must be validated in the pilot.

| Driver | Low | Base | High | Basis / reasoning | Assumption flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cycle-time reduction**, below-threshold & framework call-offs (procedure start to award) | −25% | −45% | −60% | High is set by the Prozorro Market (−58% to −67%, digital vs digital). Low reflects that only part of the cycle (clarification, negotiation, evaluation) is automated and that Estonia already runs fully electronic procedures. Prozorro core (5–6 days faster) and the KONEPS evaluation step (−93%) bracket individual steps. | A1: Estonia's below-threshold baseline duration is unknown (#2). A2: legal minimum time limits stay unchanged and cap the gain. A3: the share of procedures that are standardisable/catalogue-like is about 40–70%. |
| **Buyer effort per procedure** (officer person-days) | −20% | −35% | −50% | KONEPS evaluation step −93%. Walmart negotiations need no buyer time when the envelope holds. Officer review and signature remain (L2/L3 autonomy). | A4: the officer keeps approval and signature work. A5: escalation rate out of the envelope is 10–30%. Baseline in #3. |
| **Price effect of agent negotiation** on addressable below-threshold spend | 0.5% | 1.5% | 3.0% | Walmart pilot 1.5%, scaled 3%. Lewis-Faupel finds a null price effect for e-procurement. Kovalchuk finds 3.5–5.8% for above-threshold competitive tenders. The Fazekas & Blum 5–10% is a ceiling for full reforms. Estonia awards 83% of procedures on lowest price, so negotiation headroom on price is limited. | A6: savings are measured against the counterfactual price, not against the buyer's estimate. **Headline for the sponsor:** borrow time and effort gains with confidence. Treat price gains as small (about 1.5%). Treat disputes and SME uplift as upside scenarios that the pilot must evidence.

## Caveats on borrowing

- **Digital vs paper.** Most headline e-procurement benefits (KONEPS USD 8bn, Portugal, EC 5–20%) come from moving off paper. Estonia moved off paper by 2018, so only **digital-to-digital deltas** (Prozorro Market vs Prozorro open tender) transfer.
- **"Savings" definitions differ.** Prozorro and Prozorro Market report savings against the buyer's expected value, which overstates real savings. Consip compares framework prices with buyers' own purchases. The value model needs a single counterfactual definition (#7).
- **Benefits can erode.** In Ukraine, wartime exemptions pushed 88% of 2025 procedures outside the e-system [S6]. Gains need legal mandate and monitoring KPIs (#8).
- **Private-sector negotiation does not translate one-to-one.** Walmart could choose which suppliers to approach and could trade any variable. Public buyers must treat all suppliers equally, publish the criteria and allow challenges.
- **Some sources are paywalled or binary PDFs.** The Walmart figures (HBR, paywalled) and the Kovalchuk et al. coefficients (PDF) were cross-checked via secondary summaries [S5][S14]. Re-verify them against the original tables before the white paper.

## Open uncertainties

1. **No public-sector agent-to-agent negotiation has published measured outcomes.** Every agent analogue is private sector (Walmart, Maersk) or AI-assist (Ukraine, Chile, Brazil). Legal constraints on negotiating below-threshold procedures in Estonia (what may be negotiated, and with whom) set how much of the Walmart effect transfers.
2. **Estonia's below-threshold cycle-time baseline.** The scoreboard's 59 days covers TED-published (above-threshold) procedures only (#2).
3. **The original KONEPS build cost (2002) and Prozorro's run cost** were not found in primary sources.
4. **Re-verification** of the HBR and CGD coefficients against the original documents, which were paywalled or not machine-readable here.
5. **Dispute effects of full log transparency.** The direction is unknown and could be negative at first (#5).
