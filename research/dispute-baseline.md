# Dispute baseline: review procedures, outcomes and their cost (Estonia)

Ticket: #5 (map #1). Research date: 13 Nov 2026. Latest full year published: **2025**.
Audience: sponsor (RIK Director + Ministry of Finance) and the capstone white paper.

- VAKO: Riigihangete vaidlustuskomisjon, the Public Procurement Review Committee. It sits in the Ministry of Finance.

## Answer

**Volume.** Estonia's review committee (VAKO) received **299 review applications in 2025**. The trend is rising: 171 in 2022, 209 in 2023, 258 in 2024 and 299 in 2025. The Ministry's own indicator, the share of procurements challenged, was **2.1% in 2025**. That is just above its 1.5–2% target band. [V]

**Evaluation and award disputes.** These are the largest slice:
- "Tender declared successful" (the award decision) drew 147 objections in 2025.
- Conformity, rejection and award objections together drew 278 objections.
- Procurement-document objections drew only 54.
- Objections to the published evaluation criteria were tiny: 3–6 a year.

Roughly **30–57% of disputes (about 90–170 a year) concern evaluation/award-stage decisions**. This range is [C] from counts of objections, not of challenges.

**Outcomes.**
- About **24% of all filings succeed** in full or in part: 71 of 299 in 2025.
- About **34–41% of cases heard on the merits succeed**.
- A further ~15% of filings end because the contracting authority itself withdraws or corrects the decision. These are hidden "wins" for the challenger.
- About **13–16% of filings go to the administrative court**. VAKO decisions largely survive there: 84% become final without appeal, and 97% stand after court review.

**Delay.**
- VAKO decides in **19–22 days on average**. The statutory cap is **30 days**, or 35 days if the application is amended (RHS § 200(1)).
- After a decision on the merits, the contract cannot be signed for another **14 days** (§ 201(2)). The wait is 7 days after other terminations (§ 201(3)).
- If a party appeals, signing is blocked until the court ruling takes effect (§ 201(3¹)). Courts took about 35 days at the administrative court and about 38 days at the circuit court in 2024.
- **Typical added delay to contract signature is therefore about 4–6 weeks per dispute.** It is about 2–4 months when a dispute goes to court.

**Cost.**
- The filing fee **doubled on 1 Jan 2026**, to **€1,280 / €2,560** (below / at or above the EU threshold).
- VAKO's labour cost is about **€0.33M a year**, roughly **€1,300 per filing**. Fee revenue now covers about half of it.
- **No public source estimates** contracting-authority staff time, bidder legal cost or cost of delay.
- Using flagged assumptions, the full direct cost is **about €8k per dispute (range €4k–€22k)**. That is **about €2.5M a year (range €1.0M–€7.3M)** for about 300 disputes, before any delay cost.

**Implication for the business case [I].** "Evaluation disputes down" is a valid measure, but the baseline is small and concentrated **above** the pilot scope:
- Disputes are economically rare where the fee exceeds ~2% of contract value (contracts ≤ €50k).
- Only ~7–8 disputes a year concern procurements below the simplified threshold.
- Half of challenged procurements (99 of 199 in 2025) are above the international threshold.

So a below-threshold pilot will barely move the national dispute count. Value from dispute reduction depends on scaling the Evaluation Agent to above-threshold procedures.

Notes on the table:
- **Objections are not challenges.** One application can contest several decisions. So the subject counts (484 objections in 2025) exceed the number of applications (299). The 30–57% share is an approximation. VAKO does not publish outcomes split by subject.

Note: the Ministry's English web page still says 5 working days for simple procedures. The 2026 consolidated Act says 3 [V]. Use the Act.

[I] Whether filing an application, without a suspension order, legally bars signing *during* VAKO review is not stated in one clause. However, § 189(1) ("not after the award"), § 193(5) (leave to sign must be sought) and § 201 together mean that in practice the award is frozen from filing until 7–14 days after the decision. Confirm with VAKO or Ministry legal staff.

## 4. Assumptions to validate (all flagged [A] above)

1. **Contracting-authority staff time per dispute: 2 / 5 / 10 person-days**, at a loaded day-rate of €300 / €380 / €450. Validate with RTK, Transpordiamet and RKIK case logs.
2. **Bidder legal cost per dispute: €2k / €5k / €15k.** Validate with the Estonian Bar Association, a sample of § 198 cost awards in VAKO decisions, or the challengers who file most often.
3. **Delay cost** as opportunity cost on contract value at 2–5% a year. This is illustrative only. A better method is to value the delayed benefit per contract type.
4. **Evaluation/award share of disputes = 30–57%**, taken from objection counts rather than per-challenge data.
5. **Volume range 260–340 for the business-case horizon.** The 2026 fee doubling (to cut unfounded challenges) and the RHS amendment that raises the simplified thresholds may reduce volumes. The amendment's 01.09.2026 in-force date is reported in the Ministry's 2025 summary and was not re-verified. Neither effect has data yet.
6. The filing fee is a transfer between bidder and state, not an economic cost. It is excluded from the direct-cost total.

## 5. Open uncertainties

- **Per-segment split is not published.** Disputes are not split between below-threshold buys, framework call-offs (the pilot scope) and above-threshold tenders. What we have is that 99 of 199 challenged procurements (2025) were above the international threshold, and only ~7–8 a year are below the simplified threshold (Ministry memorandum). Ask VAKO or the register team for a 2023–2025 extract by procedure type and estimated value.
- **No outcome by subject.** We cannot say whether award-decision challenges succeed more or less often than others.
- **Inconsistent court counts.** 2024 appeals are 34 (VAKO), 24 or 26 (Ministry memo). Court outcomes for 2024–2025 were taken at early cut-off dates and will change.
- **Restated Ministry KPI series.** The 2023 and 2024 "challenged by value" figures were restated to 19%. The 2025 total procurement value reads €5.1B in one chart and ">€8B" in the text. Don't quote the value series until reconciled.
- **No public cost-of-dispute study** exists for Estonia (none found from Riigikontroll, the OECD or the EC).
- **"25,000 procedures a year" in the case study is not supported.** The register shows ~9,000 procurements and ~14,900 contracts a year. Use ~9,000 as the dispute denominator. (This also affects the other denominators in the business case.)
- **Causality.** The Ministry attributes rising disputes to higher procurement volume and complexity and to more bidder awareness, not to evaluation quality. An Evaluation Agent can plausibly affect reasoning and traceability of award decisions. It does not change bidders' incentive to litigate high-value awards. A "disputes down" target should use the Ministry's existing KPI (share of procurements challenged, target 1.5–2%) and/or award-decision objections per 1,000 procedures, measured only within the pilot scope, against a matched control.
