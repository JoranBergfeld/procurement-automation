# Competition evidence: SME participation, bidder counts and price effects

Ticket: #4 · Map: #1 · Audience: sponsor (RIK Director, Ministry of Finance) and capstone white paper
Status: resolved. Research date: May 2026. All figures are the latest public figures found. Every number has a source, and every inference is labelled **[A#]** (assumption) or **[D#]** (derived by us).

## TL;DR answer

1. **"More bidders lowers prices" is well supported, but the effect gets smaller as bidder numbers rise.** The best recent European estimate is from Denmark's competition authority (KFST 2024): each extra bid cuts the contract price by **about 2.5–3% on average**. Going from 1 to 4 bids cuts it by **about 10–13%**, and going beyond 5–6 bids adds only **about 1%** more. EU-wide, single-bid contracts cost **about 9.6% more** than contracts with several bids (Fazekas, DG REGIO 2019).
2. **Estonia already gets a fairly high average number of bidders, but its tail of uncompetitive tenders is large.**
   - The register average is **4.4 bidders per procurement in 2025**, against the Ministry's target of 4.0 for 2025 and 4.7 for 2035.
   - Above-threshold (TED) tenders receive a **single bid 28% of the time**. That is red on the EU Scoreboard and level with the EU typical value.
   - **13%** of TED procedures (and **8%** in the national register) run with **no call for bids**.
   - **83–87%** of procedures are awarded on **price only**.
   - The money is in the tail: turning single-bid and unadvertised awards into competed awards with 2 or more bids. Adding one more bidder to an already competitive tender is worth much less.
3. **SME participation is already very high in Estonia by count, and much lower by value.**
   - SMEs submit **94% of bids** (Scoreboard 2024) and won **about 80% of above-threshold contracts by number, but only about 45.5% by value** (EC 2019 study, 2011–2017).
   - So "more SME participation" lowers prices **only to the extent that it adds bidders** to tenders that currently get few (larger, value-heavy lots). Raising the SME share of bids, which is already about 94%, will not by itself lower prices.
4. **The case study's claim that "SMEs are dropping out because of response cost" is plausible but not proven for Estonia.**
   - EU audit and survey evidence says administrative burden deters bidders and hits SMEs hardest (ECA 2023). The cost of preparing a bid is material: **about €3.9k per bid** in 2009 prices [D1].
   - No Estonian dataset was found that shows SMEs dropping out over time, or shows that response cost is the reason. The Estonian average bidder count actually **rose** from 3.6 to 4.4 between 2022 and 2025.
   - One rigorous US study (Kang & Miller, QJE 2022) found administrative hurdles explain **little** of low competition.
   - Treat this claim as a **hypothesis to validate**, not a premise of the business case.
5. **Effect size for the business case** (price reduction on the affected spend; details in §3):

   | Mechanism | Low | Base | High |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | +1 bidder on a typical Estonian tender (about 4.4 → 5.4) | −0.5% | −1.5% | −2.5% |
   | Single bid → 2 or more bids (per converted tender) | −4.5% | −7% | −10% |
   | No call for bids → open, advertised procedure (per converted tender) | −1.2% | −3.8% | −10% |
   | **Blended, across total procurement spend (agent-to-agent scenario)** | **−0.15%** | **−1.1%** | **−2.5%** (capped) |

   The case study assumes a **2% gain**. That sits **inside the range but well above our base**, if price competition is the only source of the gain. Reaching 2% needs either a large reduction in single-bid and no-call procedures, or other savings (process cost, better specifications), which are out of scope for this ticket.

## 2. Price vs number of bidders: what the evidence says

| Study | Data | Finding | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| KFST (Danish Competition and Consumer Authority), Aagaard & Linaa 2024 [S8] | > 2,000 Danish EU tenders, 2015–2022 | Average **−2.5% to −3% price per extra bid**. Log price vs 1 bid: 2 bids −0.046, 3 −0.067, 4 −0.097, 5 −0.117, 6+ −0.128. 1 → 4 bids ≈ −10 to −13%; 5 → 6+ ≈ −1%. | Closest comparator: Nordic/Baltic-sized EU market; typical Danish tender gets just under 4 bids. Price only, quality not measured. |
| Fazekas (2019) for DG REGIO, citing Fazekas & Kocsis [S9] | EU-wide TED, 2009–2014 | **Single-bid contracts cost about 9.6% more** than multi-bid ones. Faster buyer decisions go with 5–30 pp less single bidding. | Relative price = contract value / estimate. |
| DG MARKT 2011 evaluation (PwC/London Economics/Ecorys) [S10] | EU, 2006–2010 | Publishing a notice saves **1.2%**; an open procedure saves a further **2.6%** (≈ 3.8% total). The Commission's earlier estimate was 2.5–10% savings from EU advertising. About 20% of tenders got one bid. | Basis for the "no call for bids" row. |
| Estache & Iimi 2008, World Bank PRWP 4662 [S11] | Donor-funded infrastructure (ODA) | Price effects **level off at about 7 bidders** (roads, water) and about 3 (electricity). N = 4 → 7: roads −6.0%, electricity −10.2 to −12.4%, water −1.1%; 8.2% overall. Literature range for the level-off point: 6–8 bidders. | Developing-country works; upper bound. |
| Coviello & Mariniello 2014, J Public Econ [S12] | Italian public works | More publicity → more bidders → larger rebates. The number of bidders is the channel. | Causal design (regression discontinuity). |
| ECA Special Report 28/2023 [S13] | EU single market, 2011–2021 | Single bidding rose 23.5% → 41.8%. Average bids fell 5.7 → 3.2. The Commission does not monitor prices, so the price impact of falling competition "cannot be assessed". | Shows the direction, not a price elasticity. |

**Synthesis (rule of thumb used below):**
- About **−2.5% per extra bidder** on average, strongly diminishing as bidder numbers rise.
- About **−4.5 to −10%** for going from 1 bid to 2 or more.
- About **−1 to −2%** for one extra bidder around Estonia's current mean of 4–5 [D3].
- About **0%** beyond roughly 6–8 bidders.

## 3. Effect size for the business case (low / base / high)

| Parameter | Low | Base | High | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price effect of +1 bidder at about 4.4 bidders | −0.5% | −1.5% | −2.5% | KFST 4→5 = −2.0%, 5→6+ = −1.1%; pooled −2.5% [S8] [D3] |
| Price effect of single bid → 2+ bids | −4.5% | −7% | −10% | KFST 1→2 = −4.6% [S8]; Fazekas 9.6% single-bid premium [S9] |
| Price effect of no call for bids → open procedure | −1.2% | −3.8% | −10% | DG MARKT 1.2% / 3.8%; Commission prior up to 10% [S10] |
| Share of spend in single-bid tenders | 10% | 18% | 28% | 28% of TED procedures by count [S1]; 18.5% in 2011–17 [S7]; value share unknown [A4] |
| Share of spend awarded without a call for bids | 3% | 8% | 13% | 8% of register procedures [S3]; 13% of TED [S1]; value share unknown [A4] |
| Conversion rate of single-bid / no-call spend to competed 2+ bids | 10% | 25% | 50% | **Pure assumption** [A5] |
| Change in average bidders on the remaining tenders | +0.2 | +0.5 | +1.0 | **Pure assumption** [A5] |

**Blended price reduction across total procurement spend [D4]:**

| Channel | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single bid → 2+ bids | 0.10 × 0.10 × 4.5% = 0.05% | 0.18 × 0.25 × 7% = 0.32% | 0.28 × 0.50 × 10% = 1.40% |
| No call → open procedure | 0.03 × 0.10 × 1.2% ≈ 0.00% | 0.08 × 0.25 × 3.8% = 0.08% | 0.13 × 0.50 × 10% = 0.65% |
| Extra bidders on the remaining tenders | 0.2 × 0.5% = 0.10% | 0.5 × 1.5% = 0.75% | 1.0 × 2.5% = 2.50% |
| **Total** | **≈ 0.15%** | **≈ 1.1%** | **4.6%, capped at 2.5%** [A6] |

Euro translation (indicative only; the business-case ticket owns the spend base):
- Applied to €5–8 bn of annual register procurement value [S3], this gives about **€8 m (low, 0.15% × €5 bn) to €200 m (high, 2.5% × €8 bn) a year**, with a base of about **€70 m (1.1% × €6.5 bn)** [A7].
- Applied only to below-threshold spend, the result scales down proportionally.
- Framework call-offs (about 56% of value) may already be competed in mini-competitions, so they **should not** be double-counted [A7].

## 4. SME participation and response cost: evidence strength

| Claim in case study | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Higher SME participation improves price competition | Only through the bidder-count channel (§2). In Estonia, SMEs already submit 94% of bids [S1]. SMEs' win share rises with competition [S7]. | **Moderate** (indirect) |
| SMEs are dropping out because of response cost | EU: more than half of survey respondents cite lengthy administrative procedures as a key deterrent; costs to bidders exceed private-sector procurement costs; SMEs with few admin staff are particularly hit; SME participation did not rise 2016–2021 [S13 ¶48, ¶58, ¶97–99]. Bid preparation is about 75% of an average procedure cost of €28k spread over 5.4 bids, so about €3.9k per bid [S10][D1]. Estonia's own monitoring form lists "high administrative burden of tendering" among possible reasons for low competition [S6]. **However**, there is no Estonian time series of SME exit, the average bidder count rose from 2022 to 2025 [S3–S5], and Kang & Miller (2022) find bidding costs (about $20–34k at the median US federal IT contract) explain only a limited part of low competition [S14]. | **Weak for Estonia**; moderate at EU level |
| A 2% price gain is achievable | Within our 0.15–2.5% range, well above the 1.1% base, for the competition channel only (§3). | **Plausible but optimistic** |

Estonian business-side signals (not procurement-specific statistics): the Chamber of Commerce (Kaubanduskoda) ran a 2025 consultation on procurement problems [S15]. In an EVEA 2025 member survey, 61% reported unreasonable administrative burden (*halduskius*) from public bodies in general [S16].

## 5. Assumptions and derived figures

- **[A1]** The Figure 2 value series in S3 has garbled row labels in the PDF. The values are read as total estimated procurement value by year and are approximate.
- **[A2]** The 2023 bidder average is inconsistent across reports: 4.1 (4.07) in S5 vs 3.6 in S4. Both are shown.
- **[A3]** The SME contract values in S6 for above-threshold contracts (e.g. €14.5 bn in 2022) exceed total annual procurement value. This looks like a data or definition error (possibly frameworks counted at maximum value), so do not use them for sizing.
- **[A4]** The Scoreboard and register shares are by count of procedures. The shares by value, and the shares below threshold, are unknown. The ranges are assumed.
- **[A5]** How much the agent-to-agent system changes bidder counts is **not evidenced anywhere**. These are scenario inputs for the sponsor to challenge.
- **[A6]** The high case is capped at 2.5% because channel effects are not additive (the same tender can be single-bid and no-call) and the marginal effect falls towards zero beyond about 6 bidders.
- **[A7]** The spend base (€5–8 bn; base €6.5 bn) and the treatment of frameworks are owned by the business-case ticket. These euro figures are illustrative only.
- **[D1]** €28,000 × 75% ÷ 5.4 bids ≈ €3,900 per bid (2009 prices, EU average, above-threshold). This is not an Estonian figure. Inflation-adjusted it is likely higher (not computed).
- **[D2]** 10+ bidder share = 100 − 18.5 − 25.2 − 36.5. The value is cut off in the PDF table.
- **[D3]** Marginal effect at Estonia's mean, interpolated from the KFST log-price coefficients. The register reports a mean, not a median. The mean is pulled up by tenders with many bids, so the typical tender likely has fewer bidders, which would make the marginal effect larger.
- **[D4]** Blended effect = Σ (spend share × conversion rate × per-tender effect) + (Δ average bidders × marginal effect).

## 6. Open uncertainties

1. **No Estonian price-vs-bidders estimate.** All elasticities are imported (Denmark, the EU, the US, the World Bank). A register micro-data regression (contract value vs estimate, by number of bids) would settle this.
2. **No Estonian evidence that SMEs are dropping out or what it costs them to respond.** A supplier survey, or a register panel of unique bidders by firm size, would be needed.
3. **The national register does not publish a below-threshold single-bid share.** Above-threshold data is 28% (TED).
4. **The case study cites 25,000 procedures a year, but the register shows about 9.1k procurements (14.9k contracts) in 2025.** Framework mini-competitions and procurements under the register threshold may explain the gap. This affects every scaled figure.
5. **Quality effects are ignored.** Price falls could come with lower quality, and 83–87% of awards are price-only.
6. **Market concentration.** In some sectors (postal/telecoms, oil and gas services), low competition is structural, so no procurement-process change will add bidders.
