# Legal negotiation envelope: what may be negotiated in below-threshold and call-off procedures

Ticket: [#11](https://github.com/JoranBergfeld/procurement-automation/issues/11) · Map: [#1](https://github.com/JoranBergfeld/procurement-automation/issues/1) · Feeds: [#9](https://github.com/JoranBergfeld/procurement-automation/issues/9) · Researched: 2026-11-13

> **Law as of 13 Nov 2026.** A major amendment to the Public Procurement Act (Riigihangete seadus, RHS) entered into force on **1 Nov 2026** ([RT I, 03.07.2026, 3][amend]). It abolished the middle "public procurement threshold" tier and raised the simple-procurement thresholds. Procedures started before 1 Nov 2026 finish under the old rules (new RHS § 219³). The official English translation on Riigi Teataja ([EN 01.09.2025 version][rhs-en]) predates the amendment, so the article text below is quoted from that translation and **adjusted by hand** for the amendment. The changes were checked against the Estonian amending act.

## Answer

Estonian law allows real, multi-round, multi-parameter negotiation in only two places that matter for the pilot:

1. **Below the simple-procurement threshold** (from 1 Nov 2026: < €50,000 for supplies/services and < €100,000 for works). No statutory procedure applies. Negotiation is limited only by the general principles (RHS § 3), the authority's own internal procurement rules (§ 9(4)(3)), and EU Treaty principles where there is a certain cross-border interest.
2. **The simple procedure** (*lihthankemenetlus*, from the simple threshold up to the EU threshold, e.g. €140,000 for central-government supplies/services in 2026–27). Negotiation is allowed **only if the procurement documents reserved it and set its conditions** (§ 125(6)). It must treat all compliant tenderers equally. The **competitive procedure with negotiation** is also available for any procurement below the EU threshold (§ 48(3)(1)). Its rules are explicit: negotiate anything except the minimum requirements and award criteria, in stages, with every tenderer still in the procedure, followed by non-negotiable final tenders (§§ 70–71).

**Framework-agreement call-offs are *not* a negotiation venue.** Without reopening competition, the contract follows the framework terms. Only a sole-supplier framework allows the supplier to be asked to "supplement" its tender (§ 30(3)). Reopening competition (a "mini-competition") is a single sealed written tender round among all capable parties. It covers only the terms the framework documents said it would cover, and it may not substantially change the framework (§ 30(1), (7), (9); Directive art. 33). The only lawful *multi-round* price improvement in a mini-competition is an **electronic auction** (§ 36(2)): automated re-bidding on price or numeric criteria, with tenderers kept anonymous to each other.

**Value-model consequence:** private-sector "agent negotiates bilaterally with each supplier" gains (Walmart/Pactum-style, see #6) carry over **only** to below-threshold buys and, in a weaker form, to simple or negotiated procedures that reserve negotiation. For framework call-offs, the legally reachable gain comes from **competition intensity** (more SME bids, faster mini-competitions, e-auctions), not from bargaining. If the value model assumes bargaining in call-offs, that assumption is legally unsupported.

## Thresholds that define the envelope (in force from 1 Nov 2026)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Below simple threshold | < €50,000 | < €100,000 | < €500,000 / < €100,000 | RHS § 14(1) as amended by [amend] items 5–8 |
| Simple procedure (§ 125) | €50,000 to the EU threshold | €100,000 to the EU threshold | special § 126 regime | RHS § 15(1) as amended by [amend] item 10 |
| EU ("international") threshold 2026–27 | €140,000 (central government) / €216,000 (sub-central) | €5,404,000 | €750,000 (Directive art. 4(d), unchanged) | [Reg. (EU) 2025/2152][thr] |

The old €60,000 / €150,000 "riigihanke piirmäär" tier is repealed (§ 14(2) repealed, [amend] item 9). The State Gazette summary says the reform simplifies about 49% of procurements and moves them from an average of 87 days to about 50 days ([summary][amend-sum]).

## Negotiation envelope by procedure type

| Procedure | Legal basis | What may be negotiated | With whom | Rounds | Key constraints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Below simple threshold** (small purchase, *väikeost*) | RHS § 3, § 9(4)(3) (internal rules); EU primary law only if certain cross-border interest ([Telaustria][c324], [SECAP][c147]) | Everything: price, delivery, terms, specification. The Act sets no procedure. | Any operators the authority chooses; one or several | Unlimited (the § 45(7¹) ban on viewing tenders before the deadline applies only from the simple threshold upwards) | Transparency, verifiability, proportionality, equal treatment, competition, economy (§ 3). Internal procurement rules may impose more (e.g. "3 quotes"). Contestable before VAKO under the simple-procedure time limits (§ 189(4¹) as amended). |
| **Simple procedure** (*lihthankemenetlus*) | RHS § 125, esp. § 125(2), (5), (6) | Whatever the procurement documents declare negotiable. The Act does not expressly forbid negotiating minimum requirements, but equal treatment and transparency (§ 3) and CJEU case law ([Nordecon][c561], [Walloon buses][c87]) make it unsafe. Best practice is to borrow the § 70 rule (no negotiation of minimum requirements or award criteria). | All tenderers whose tenders are compliant, on equal terms | Free design ("successive stages" allowed), **but only if foreseen in the documents together with its conditions** (§ 125(6)) | Contract notice in the register. Tender period ≥ 15 working days (supplies/services) or 25 (works) since 1 Nov 2026 ([amend] item 77). 7-working-day standstill ([amend] item 80). Award criteria per § 85. E-auction allowed (§ 36(2)). |
| **Competitive procedure with negotiation** (allowed below the EU threshold without further justification) | RHS § 48(3)(1), §§ 67–71; Directive [2014/24 art. 29][dir] | Content of the initial and later tenders: price, delivery, terms, technical solution. **Not** the minimum requirements or the award criteria (§ 70(2), (6¹); art. 29(3)). Non-compliant tenders cannot be negotiated into compliance ([Nordecon C-561/12][c561]). | Selected candidates (minimum 3 if the authority limits numbers, § 68(3)); all tenderers not yet eliminated | Multiple stages. Numbers may be reduced stage by stage using the published award criteria, if pre-announced (§ 70(6)). Then final tenders with **no negotiation** (§ 71(1)). Written, simultaneous notice of any changes to all remaining tenderers (§ 70(6¹)). |
| **Negotiated procedure without prior publication** | RHS §§ 49, 50 (extra grounds below the EU threshold, e.g. food, § 50(9)), § 72; § 125(3) lets the simple procedure be run this way | Terms of the contract (§ 72(6)). The original terms may not be substantially modified where the ground is a failed procedure (§ 49(1)(1)). | One or several invited operators (§ 72(1)) | Free | Only on an exhaustive statutory ground. Exceptional, so not a scalable channel. |
| **Framework call-off without reopening competition** | RHS § 30(1), (3), (6)(1), (8); Directive [art. 33(3)–(4)(a)][dir] | **Nothing substantive.** The contract follows the framework terms. With a single-supplier framework the authority may ask the supplier to "supplement its indicative tender" (§ 30(3)). | The framework party (cascade or rule set in the framework) | n/a | No substantial amendment of the framework (§ 30(1); [pressetext C-454/06][c454]). The framework must state the maximum quantity or value ([Coopservice C-216/17][c216]; [Simonsen & Weel C-23/20][c23]). § 30(8) "free choice" call-offs: each below the simple threshold (now €50k) and ≤ 20% of the framework value in total, on framework terms. |
| **Framework call-off with reopened competition (mini-competition)** | RHS § 30(4)–(7), (9), (11); § 31 (voidness); Directive [art. 33(5)–(6)][dir] | Only the terms the framework documents said the reopening may cover (§ 30(7)), "more precisely formulated" (art. 33(5)). Typically price, delivery, specific quantities and service levels. | **All** framework parties able to perform (§ 30(9)(1)) | **One** sealed written round. Tenders are opened after the deadline when the call-off is at or above the simple threshold (§ 30(9)(3¹)). No negotiation provision. **E-auction allowed** for multi-round numeric improvement (§ 36(2)). | MEAT under the pre-announced criteria (§ 30(9)(4)). Optional 7-day standstill (§ 30(11)). A breach of the § 30(9) reopening rules makes the call-off **void** (§ 31(1)). |
| **Dynamic purchasing system call-off** | RHS §§ 32–35 | No negotiation. Restricted-procedure logic. | All admitted participants | One round (plus optional e-auction) | Equal deadline for all if agreed (§ 35(3) as amended). |
| **Electronic auction** (overlay on simple, open, restricted, competitive-with-negotiation, DPS or mini-competition) | RHS §§ 36–38; Directive art. 35 | Price and/or numerically expressed award criteria only (§ 36(4)). Not intellectual services that cannot be ranked automatically (§ 36(3)). | All tenderers with suitable tenders, invited simultaneously (§ 37(3)) | Multiple stages (§ 37(7)) | The formula is disclosed in advance (§ 37(4)). Real-time rank visible. **Identities never disclosed** (§ 37(9)). Starts ≥ 2 working days after invitation (§ 37(6)). |

**Open and restricted procedures** (the default above the EU threshold, § 48(1)) do **not allow negotiation**. Only equal clarification of tenders is permitted ([SAG ELV C-599/10][c599]; Ministry of Finance innovation-procurement guide, [ch. 6][mof-inno]: "Avatud ja piiratud hankemenetluses on läbirääkimised rangelt keelatud").

## Cross-cutting rules the protocol must encode (the "envelope")

1. **Pre-commitment.** The negotiable parameters, the negotiation method, stage reductions, e-auction formulas and reopening scope must be fixed **in the published procurement documents before tenders are submitted** (§ 125(6), § 70(6), § 30(7), § 37(4)). An agent may not invent a negotiation lever at run time.
2. **Fixed floor.** Minimum requirements and award criteria are immutable once published (§ 70(2), (6¹); art. 29(3)). Non-compliant offers cannot be "negotiated in" ([Nordecon][c561]).
3. **Equal treatment of information.** Any change or clarification goes simultaneously and in writing to every remaining tenderer (§ 70(6¹)). One tenderer's data is not leaked to others (§ 70(5)). A concession offered by the buyer to one tenderer must be offered to all ([Walloon buses C-87/94][c87]).
4. **Closed final round.** Negotiation ends with formal notice, followed by final tenders with no further negotiation (§ 71(1)).
5. **Written, reproducible record.** Communication is electronic from the simple threshold upwards (§ 45(1) as amended). The procurement report and the contestation right (§§ 185–201) require a reproducible log. This supports the case study's "Audit & Transparency Agent".
6. **No post-award bargaining.** Contract changes are limited to § 123 / art. 72 (the substantial-modification test, [pressetext][c454]).
7. **Human award decision.** RHS assigns award decisions to the contracting authority. Nothing in RHS authorises an automated award, so the officer signs (consistent with the case study's L2 Evaluation Agent).

## Implications for the value model

| Spend segment | Negotiation mechanism legally available | Can private-sector agent-negotiation gains carry over? | Suggested treatment in the model (assumption, flag) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below simple threshold (< €50k / < €100k) | Free bilateral or multilateral negotiation within § 3 and internal rules | **Yes, closest analogue to Walmart/Pactum** (many small, low-touch suppliers) | Apply the analogue savings rate from #6, discounted for public-sector constraints (equal treatment means the same offer must go to all, and the log is published). **A1** |
| Simple procedure, negotiation reserved | Multi-round, all compliant tenderers, fixed floor | Partly. Price and terms can improve, but no bilateral "deal-making" with only one tenderer | Apply a reduced share of the analogue rate, only to the share of procedures that reserve negotiation (unknown, see uncertainty 7). **A2** |
| Competitive procedure with negotiation (below the EU threshold) | Structured staged negotiation | Partly, as above, but heavier process | Same as A2. Treat as an opt-in for complex buys. |
| Framework call-off, no reopening | None | **No** | Model cycle-time and cost-per-procedure gains only. **Zero negotiated price gain.** |
| Framework mini-competition | One sealed round, optional e-auction | **No bargaining gain**. Gain comes from more bidders (SME participation) and e-auction price pressure. | Model the "competition intensity" effect (bidders per call-off → price) separately from negotiation. **A3** |
| Above the EU threshold, open or restricted | None | No | Out of scope for negotiation value. |

**Bottom line for the sponsor:** the headline "2% of below-threshold spend" (case study) should be decomposed into (a) process efficiency (cycle time, officer hours, SME response cost), which applies everywhere, and (b) negotiated price gain, which is legally reachable only in segments 1–3. The value model should never credit negotiated price gains to framework call-offs.

### Assumptions flagged

- **A1.** Equal-treatment and transparency obligations below the simple threshold reduce, but do not eliminate, the private-sector negotiation uplift. *No primary source quantifies this.*
- **A2.** Contracting authorities will actually reserve negotiation in their simple-procedure documents. Currently a policy choice, not a legal default.
- **A3.** Mini-competition price gains are driven by bidder count and e-auctions, not negotiation. Literature to be sourced in the value-model ticket.
- **A4.** The case study's "central procurement agency" is assumed to act as a central purchasing body (in Estonia this is Riigi Tugiteenuste Keskus, RTK; RIK is the sponsor's IT agency). Its frameworks are assumed to be multi-supplier with mini-competitions.
- **A5.** EU-threshold figures assume central-government contracting authorities (€140k). Sub-central buyers (municipalities) use €216k, which widens the simple-procedure band.

## Open uncertainties

1. **No consolidated English text after 1 Nov 2026 yet.** Section references combine the 2025 translation with the amending act. They should be re-checked against the consolidated Estonian text when a lawyer reviews this.
2. **Negotiating minimum requirements in the simple procedure.** § 125 does not import § 70(2). Whether VAKO would allow minimum requirements to change during simple-procedure negotiations (with notice to all) is untested in the sources found. Treat it as forbidden.
3. **No VAKO or Riigikohus ruling on automated or agent-run negotiation** was found. Whether an AI agent's concessions are attributable to the contracting authority, and how logs serve as the procurement record, are open questions.
4. **§ 30(8) free-choice call-offs.** Whether the buyer may solicit a price *below* framework terms from the chosen party (an improvement, not a substantial modification) is not settled in the sources reviewed.
5. **Below-threshold internal rules vary by authority** (§ 9). Each ministry or municipality may cap negotiation (e.g. by requiring a fixed number of written quotes). The pilot needs an inventory of these rules.
6. **EU AI Act classification** of a public buyer's negotiation agent was not assessed here.
7. **Spend and procedure-count split by band** (below simple threshold / simple procedure / framework call-off with and without reopening), and the share of simple procedures that reserve negotiation, is unknown. It is needed to weight the value model (proposed as a new ticket).
