# Procurement baseline: Estonian volumes, spend and cycle times (latest figures)

Ticket: #2 (map #1). Research date: November 2026. The latest complete year is **2025**.
Audience: the sponsor (Ministry of Finance, plus whichever agency owns delivery; see §5) and the capstone white paper.

## 1. Answer (TL;DR)

- **Spend.** Estonian general-government procurement is about **14.8% of GDP**. That is roughly **EUR 5.9bn in 2024** and an estimated **EUR 6.2bn in 2025** (OECD). The case study's "15% of GDP" claim **holds**.
- **Volume.** About **9,100 registered procurement procedures** were run nationally in 2025, leading to **14,850 contracts**. The central purchasing body (RTK) ran **457** of them. The case study's claim of "25,000 procedures a year at the central agency" is **false**. It overstates the central agency by about 55× and the whole country by about 2.7×.
- **Below-EU-threshold spend.** We estimate **EUR 1.1–2.2bn a year (base about EUR 1.5–1.7bn)**. A 2% efficiency gain would therefore be worth **EUR 22–45M (base about EUR 31–34M)**.
  - The claim of "2% ⇒ > EUR 30M" is **plausible but marginal**. It only clears EUR 30M at our base case, and that base case depends on an unsourced assumption about unregistered small purchases.
  - If the scope is limited to procedures where negotiation is legally allowed *today*, 2% is worth only **EUR 13–25M**.
- **Cycle time.** The latest average from publication to contract signature is **47 days for simplified procedures and 72 days for open procedures** (2025).
  - A 60% cut would mean about **19 and 29 days**. That is at or below the statutory floor (minimum tender periods plus standstill), and it is not reachable for open procedures.
- **Baseline is moving.** A procurement-law (RHS) reform takes effect on **1 Nov 2026**. It raises the simplified-procedure thresholds and abolishes the national threshold. On its own, it already moves about 49% of procedures from about 87 days to about 50 days. Benefits should be measured against this post-reform baseline, not against 2025.

Thresholds:

- **Before 1 Nov 2026** [S7]:
  - simplified procedure from EUR 30k for goods and services, EUR 60k for works;
  - national-threshold procedure from EUR 60k for goods and services, EUR 150k for works, EUR 300k for social services.
- **From 1 Nov 2026** [S8]:
  - the simplified procedure starts at EUR 50k for goods and services, EUR 100k for works, EUR 500k for social services;
  - the national threshold is abolished.
- **EU thresholds:**
  - 2024–25: EUR 143k central / 221k sub-central for goods and services, EUR 5.538M for works;
  - 2026–27: EUR 140k / 216k / 5.404M [S9].

## 3. Method for the register-derived (R) figures

We downloaded the procurement register's 2025 open data. The source is twelve monthly eForms XML files from `https://riigihanked.riik.ee/rhr/api/public/v1/opendata/notice/2025/month/{m}/xml` [S10], about 40 MB each.

1. **Parsing.** We read 14,213 contract-notice versions and kept the last version per procurement folder. That leaves **9,228 procedures**: 8,500 regular procedures plus 728 grant-recipient purchases.
2. **Reconciliation with the Ministry's count.** 8,500 + 704 procedures without prior publication (which have no contract notice) = 9,204. That is within 1% of the MoF figure of 9,137 [S3].
3. **Reconciliation with the Ministry's value.** The total estimated value, excluding grant recipients, is EUR 5.38bn, against EUR 5.11bn in the MoF figure. So the open data reproduces the official totals.
4. **Missing values.** Only 3,680 of the 8,500 regular procedures publish an estimated value.
   - Among those that do, **2,700 are below the EU threshold, worth EUR 529.8M**. We classed a procedure as below the threshold if it was under EUR 221k for goods and services, EUR 5.538M for works, or EUR 750k for social services.
   - Of these, EUR 107M (20%) are framework agreements.
   - By procedure type: open EUR 383M, simplified EUR 80M, social/special services EUR 45M, small EUR 7M.
5. **Imputation for the 4,820 procedures with no published value.** We applied each type's below-threshold mean, as follows.

| Type | Count without value | Assumption | Mean used | Imputed value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Simplified | 1,260 | – | EUR 106k | EUR 134M |
| Small | 1,350 | – | EUR 27k | EUR 36M |
| Open | 1,963 | 64.6% are below threshold, the same share as in valued open procedures | EUR 263k | EUR 334M |
| Social/special | 117 | – | EUR 223k | EUR 26M |
| **Total** | | | | **EUR 530M** |

   So the registered below-EU-threshold total is about **EUR 1.06bn**. We use ±25% for the low/high range because the imputation is uncertain.
6. **Annualisation (A2).** We assume below-threshold framework agreements last 3 years on average. Their value is then counted at one-third, which reduces the total by 20% × 2/3 ≈ 13%. That gives about EUR 0.92bn.

## 4. Sanity check of the case study's headline claims

| # | Claim (docs/case-study16.md) | Arithmetic | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "Public procurement = 15% of GDP" | OECD: 14.83% (2024), 15.26% (2023) [S2]. Gives 14.83% × EUR 41.6bn ≈ **EUR 6.2bn** in 2025. The register-value-to-GDP ratio swings between 12.3% and 22.1% over 2021–25, but that measures something else: multi-year ceilings, including state-owned companies | **Holds.** Cite the OECD figure and give the EUR amount (≈ EUR 6bn), not the register ratio |
| H2 | "25,000 procedures/yr at the central agency" | RTK: **457** (25,000 / 457 ≈ 55×). National total: **9,137** procedures (≈ 2.7× short) or **14,850** contracts. You only get to about 25k by summing contracts (14,850), DPS call-offs (~6,600), grant-recipient purchases (724) and an unknown number of mini-competitions, and those are not "procedures" | **False as stated.** Replace with "≈ 9,100 procedures and ≈ 15,000 contracts a year nationally, plus ≈ 6,600 DPS call-offs; the central purchasing body runs ≈ 460" |
| H3 | "2% efficiency on below-threshold spend > EUR 30M/yr" | Needs EUR 30M / 0.02 = **EUR 1.5bn** of below-threshold spend. Our estimate is EUR 1.1bn / 1.5–1.7bn / 2.2bn. At 2% that is **EUR 22M / 31–34M / 45M**. If the scope is limited to procedures where negotiation is legal today (simplified, small, social/special and unregistered purchases), the value is EUR 0.33bn registered + 0.31–0.93bn unregistered = **EUR 0.64–1.26bn**, and 2% = **EUR 13–25M**. Open procedures do not permit negotiation | **Plausible but marginal.** It clears EUR 30M only at base or above. Under the post-reform law (simplified procedure up to the EU threshold), the negotiable scope grows to roughly the full below-EU total, which supports the base case. Present it as a **range of EUR 20–45M**, not "> EUR 30M". The 2% gain rate itself is untested (separate ticket) |
| H4 | "Cycle time −60%" | Baseline 47 days (simplified) and 72 days (open). A 60% cut gives **18.8** and **28.8** days. Estimated statutory floor for a simplified procedure: a tender period of at least 5 working days (goods and services) or 15 days (works) [S11], plus evaluation, plus a 10-day standstill below the EU threshold [S11] ⇒ **about 17–25 days at best**. Above the EU threshold, open procedures need a tender period of at least 30 days plus a 14-day standstill, so **more than 44 days** | **Over-claimed.** It is only arithmetically possible for simplified goods/services procedures, and only if evaluation and decision time fall to almost zero. It is impossible for EU-threshold open procedures. The 1 Nov 2026 reform already moves about 49% of procedures from about 87 days to about 50 days [S8], so that part of the gain belongs to the law, not the AI |

Assumptions to flag:

- **A1.** The 2025 procurement share of GDP equals the 2024 OECD value. The OECD has not yet published 2025.
- **A2.** Below-threshold framework agreements last 3 years on average.
- **A3.** Unregistered purchases below the simplified threshold are 5–15% of total procurement. This has no public source; the case relies on it most.
- **A4.** Procedures with no published value have the same below-threshold share and mean as those that publish one.
- **A5.** The estimated value of procedures started in a year approximates that year's spend. Contract-award and payment values are not used.

## 5. Ownership note (affects the sponsor framing)

The case study names the **RIK Director** as sponsor. RIK (*Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus*) is the Centre of Registers and Information Systems, and it does **not** own procurement:

- **The procurement register (riigihangete register)** is controlled by **RTK**, the State Shared Service Centre, which is also the central purchasing body. Its IT is processed by **RMIT**, the Ministry of Finance's IT centre. This has been the case since 1 Jan 2024 [S12].
- **Procurement policy and statistics** sit with the **Ministry of Finance** [S3].

The sponsor and data-access path should therefore be **MoF + RTK** (+ RMIT), or the case must explain RIK's role.

## 7. Open uncertainties

1. **The 2025 value is unstable.** The MoF report says both "> EUR 8bn" and EUR 5.1bn. The 2024 value was revised up by 37% a year later. Expect 2025 to be revised too. *Action: ask MoF/RTK which figure is authoritative.*
2. **Below-threshold spend is not published.**
   - Unregistered purchases below EUR 30k/60k (A3) are invisible in the register. They might be visible in State Treasury e-invoice or ledger data (RTK runs the shared accounting).
   - This is the single biggest swing factor for H3.
3. **Estimated value vs spend.** The register records estimated contract value at launch, including multi-year framework ceilings. Contract-award values and actual payments would give a truer annual figure.
4. **Mini-competition counts** inside framework agreements are not published, though they are likely to be the main transaction volume for an agent system.
5. **Scope mismatch.** The register includes state-owned and network-sector companies (Elektrilevi, Enefit), while the OECD figure is general government only. The two totals are not additive or directly comparable.
6. **The cycle-time metric** runs from publication to contract signature. Preparation time before publication, which is likely where AI saves the most, is not measured anywhere public.
7. **The post-reform baseline.** No data exists yet on cycle times under the 1 Nov 2026 thresholds. The first readout would be the 2026/2027 MoF overview.
