# Public Procurement Business Case

The terms used to assess the value of agent-to-agent public procurement.

## Language

**No-agent counterfactual**:
The procurement system and outcomes expected without the agent-to-agent programme, against which programme costs and benefits are assessed.

**Freed officer capacity**:
Procurement-officer time made available when procurement work requires less human effort. It does not by itself reduce government spending.

**Realised cash saving**:
Government spending demonstrably reduced or avoided relative to the no-agent counterfactual.

**Contract-price saving**:
A reduction in what a public buyer pays relative to the no-agent counterfactual, adjusted for quantity and quality. It can be a transfer from suppliers rather than an economy-wide gain.

**Government financial view**:
A perspective on procurement programme costs and benefits that accrue to government bodies. It excludes benefits that accrue only to suppliers.

**Economy-wide view**:
A perspective on procurement programme costs and benefits across affected parties, including government and suppliers.

**Full-flow lead time**:
Elapsed time from the buyer's recorded decision to initiate a procurement until the last required contract signature.
_Avoid_: cycle time, when the start point is unstated

**Publication-to-signature time**:
Elapsed time from first publication of a tender notice until the last required contract signature. Not applicable to procurements without publication.

**Handover**:
The point at which a step is ready for the next responsible actor, human or agent, to take over.

**Handover latency**:
Elapsed time from a step becoming ready for its next responsible actor until that actor starts work. Excludes statutory waiting periods and supplier response periods.

**Negotiation envelope**:
The recorded set of rules and bounds within which a permitted negotiation may take place. Applies only where negotiation is lawful.

**Guardrail**:
A measure showing whether procurement is carried out safely and fairly, as opposed to a measure of speed or effort.

**Human escalation**:
A procurement matter referred to an authorised person for a decision, with a recorded reason and visible status.

**Audit record**:
The applicable, attributable evidence of what happened and what decisions were taken in a procurement or call-off.

**Measurement coverage**:
The extent to which the observations needed for a KPI are available across its applicable population.

**Supplier agent**:
An agent acting on behalf of a prospective supplier in a procurement, under the supplier's control rather than the public buyer's.

**Supplier-agent intervention**:
A human action needed to unblock, correct, or take over a supplier-agent response, distinct from ordinary planned review or approval.
