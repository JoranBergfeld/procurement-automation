# Business case evidence: agent-to-agent public procurement in Estonia

Evidence pack for the business case of [Case 16](case-study16.md). Each `##` section is one slide: a headline, the numbers behind it, and speaker notes with the caveats.

- **Status:** research phase complete. The value model, KPIs and targets are still open decisions on the [wayfinder map](https://github.com/JoranBergfeld/procurement-automation/issues/1).
- **Scope:** the full production system, not just the pilot. The pilot only has to show feasibility.
- **Conventions:** ranges are low / base / high. **[A]** marks an assumption, not a measured figure. EUR, 2025 prices, unless noted.

---

## 1. The case study's headline claims, checked

**Headline:** the size of the prize is real, but three of the four headline claims need rewording before they reach a sponsor.

| Case study claim | What the evidence says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Procurement is 15% of GDP | 14.8% (OECD 2024) × €41.6bn GDP ≈ **€6.2bn/yr** | ✅ Holds |
| 25,000 procedures/yr at the central agency | **~9,100 procedures** nationally (~14,850 contracts); the central purchasing body (RTK) ran **457** | ❌ False as stated |
| 2% gain on below-threshold spend > €30M/yr | **€22–45M** (base €31–34M); only **€13–25M** where negotiation is legal today | ⚠️ Marginal |
| Cycle time −60% | Today 47 days (simple) / 72 days (open). −60% gives 19 / 29 days, at or below the legal minimum | ⚠️ Over-claimed |

**Speaker notes**

- "25,000" only appears if you add contracts (14,850) + DPS call-offs (~6,600) + grant-recipient purchases (724) + mini-competitions. Those are transactions, not procedures.
- The case names the RIK Director as sponsor, but RIK has no procurement role. The register is run by **RTK** (State Shared Service Centre) with the Ministry of Finance IT centre; policy sits with the **Ministry of Finance**.

## 2. The baseline is moving: the 1 Nov 2026 reform

**Headline:** a law change already delivers part of the speed gain, so the business case must not claim it for the agents.

| | Before 1 Nov 2026 | From 1 Nov 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Small purchase (no statutory procedure) | < €30k goods/services, < €60k works | **< €50k** goods/services, **< €100k** works |
| National threshold tier | €60k / €150k | **Abolished** |
| Simple procurement (*lihthange*) | €30k/€60k up to national threshold | **€50k/€100k up to the EU threshold** |
| EU threshold (2026–27) | €140k central / €216k sub-central (goods, services); €5.404M works | Unchanged |

- ~**49% of procedures** move to the simpler route.
- Their average duration drops from **~87 to ~50 days** (Ministry estimate).

**Speaker notes:** every benefit in the business case is measured against this post-reform baseline, not against 2025.

---

## 3. What "below the EU threshold" means

**Headline:** below the EU threshold, Estonia sets its own rules. That is where it can pilot agent negotiation without changing EU law.

- The EU Procurement Directive (2014/24/EU) only applies to contracts at or above the EU threshold.
- Above it: EU-wide publication, minimum tender periods (open procedure ≥ 30 days), a 14-day standstill, and **no negotiation** in open or restricted procedures.
- Below it: Estonian law (Riigihangete seadus) alone, with lighter procedures (simple procurement: tenders open ≥ 15 working days for goods/services, 25 for works) and a 7-working-day standstill.

| EU threshold 2026–27 | Value |
|---|---|
| Goods & services, central government | €140,000 |
| Goods & services, other public buyers | €216,000 |
| Works | €5,404,000 |
| Social & other specific services | €750,000 |

## 4. Where the procedures and the money sit

**Headline:** ~70% of procedures fall below the EU threshold, but they hold only ~15–40% of the registered value. Volume and value point to different targets.

| Band | Procedures | Registered value (low / base / high) |
|---|---|---|
| Small purchase (< €50k / €100k), registered | ~2,900 | ~€0.27bn |
| Simple procurement (up to the EU threshold) | ~3,550 | €0.8 / **1.3** / 1.8bn |
| ↳ of which works €100k–€5.4M | ~1,400 | ~€1.1bn |
| At or above the EU threshold | ~2,000 | €3.6 / **4.6** / 6.7bn |
| Grant-recipient purchases | ~730 | ~€0.37bn |
| **Registered total** | **~9,140** | **€5.1bn** (first estimate; expect €5.4–7.0bn after revision) |
| Small purchases never registered **[A]** | ≫ 10,000 transactions | €0.3 / 0.6 / 0.9bn of *spend* |

| Framework call-offs (not counted as procedures) | Count | Share of call-off value |
|---|---|---|

**Speaker notes**

- Frameworks are ~23% of procedures but **56% of value**.
- Goods and services below the EU threshold are only ~€0.3bn of registered value; works dominate the simple band.

- 57% of notices publish no value; values were imputed from similar notices **[A]**.

## 6. What one procedure costs

**Headline:** a full procedure costs about €16,500, and suppliers carry ~70% of it.

| Per full procedure (above the old national threshold) | Hours | Rate | Cost (low / base / high) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer | 128 / **160** / 280 h | €30.4/h | €3,100 / **€4,860** / €10,200 |
| Each bidder | 72 / **96** / 160 h | €26.4/h | €1,500 / **€2,530** / €6,200 |
| Winner's post-award paperwork | 14 / **19** / 32 h | €26.4/h | €300 / **€510** / €1,240 |
| **Total** (4.4 bidders) | ~400 / **~600** / ~1,200 h | | **€8,900 / €16,500 / €47,000** |

| Per simple procurement | Hours | Cost (low / base / high) |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer ~70 h + ~30 h per bidder × 4.4 | ~110 / **~200** / ~480 h | **€2,500 / €5,600 / €18,400** |

**How the number is built**

1. **Hours:** European Commission evaluation SWD(2025) 332. Buyer 20 person-days, bidder 12 person-days (EU median 2019–24), × 8 h. Simple procurement hours from Oslo Economics (Norway, below-threshold).
2. **Rates:** Eurostat 2025 employer labour cost for Estonia. Public administration €24.3/h, business economy €21.1/h, + 25% overhead (Commission method).
3. **Formula:** buyer + (4.4 bidders × bidder cost) + winner's post-award work = €4,860 + €11,130 + €510 ≈ **€16,500**.

**Speaker notes:** all hours are borrowed from EU and Nordic studies; Estonia has no time measurements of its own. Consultants and legal fees are excluded (Danish data suggests +30%). Losing bidders are assumed to work as hard as the winner.

## 7. What procurement costs Estonia per year

**Headline:** about €111M a year in transaction cost, ~€76M of it on suppliers.

| 2025 volume | Count | Cost per procedure | Total (low / base / high) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full procedures | 5,444 | €16,500 | €48M / **€90M** / €255M |
| Simple procurements | 3,693 | €5,600 | €9M / **€21M** / €68M |
| **Total** | **9,137** | | **€58M / €111M / €323M** |

| Who pays (base) | Amount | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Contracting authorities | ~€34M | ~670 full-time staff |
| Suppliers | ~€76M | |

**Speaker notes**

- This uses the **pre-reform** mix. With ~49% moving to the cheaper simple route, the total could fall by up to ~€40M before any agent is deployed.
- Excluded: mini-competitions, DPS calls, contract management, litigation.
- The case claims officers spend "most of their time" on clarification. Nothing measures this. EU data puts the largest buyer share (48–53%) in preparing needs, specifications and documents.

## 8. Competition: more bidders, lower prices, with diminishing returns

**Headline:** Estonia already gets 4.4 bids on average. The money is in the 28% of tenders with a single bid, not in adding a fifth bidder.

| Estonian indicator | Estonia | EU |
|---|---|---|
| Average bids per procedure (2025) | **4.4** (target 4.7 by 2035) | — |
| Single-bid procedures (TED, 2024) | **28%** | 28% |
| Awarded without a call for bids | 8% (register) / 13% (TED) | 5% |
| Price-only award | 83–87% | 54% |
| Bids from SMEs | **94%** | 72% |
| SME share of contract value won (2011–17) | 45.5% | — |

| Price effect (per affected tender) | Low | Base | High | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +1 bidder at ~4.4 bidders | −0.5% | −1.5% | −2.5% | Danish Competition Authority 2024 |
| Single bid → 2+ bids | −4.5% | −7% | −10% | DK 2024; Fazekas 2019 (single-bid premium 9.6%) |
| No call for bids → open procedure | −1.2% | −3.8% | −10% | DG MARKT 2011 |
| **Blended, across all procurement spend** **[A]** | **−0.15%** | **−1.1%** | **−2.5%** (capped) | Conversion rates are assumptions |

**Speaker notes**

- On €5–8bn of registered value, the blended effect is roughly €8M (low) / **€70M** (base) / €200M (high) a year.
- "SMEs drop out because of response cost" is plausible at EU level but **not proven for Estonia**: the average bid count rose from 3.6 to 4.4 between 2022 and 2025. Treat it as a hypothesis to test.
- Raising the SME share of bids (already 94%) does not lower prices on its own. Only extra bidders on thin tenders do.

## 9. Disputes: small in money, concentrated above the threshold

**Headline:** ~300 challenges a year, costing ~€2.5M directly. Disputes are a trust and speed KPI more than a money stream.

| 2025 | Value |
|---|---|
| Review applications filed | **299** (171 in 2022, rising) |
| Share of procurements challenged | 2.1% (Ministry target 1.5–2%) |
| Concerning evaluation or award decisions | ~30–57% |
| Upheld in full or in part | ~24% of filings |
| Added delay per dispute | **4–6 weeks**; 2–4 months if it goes to court |
| Filing fee (from 1 Jan 2026) | €1,280 below / €2,560 at or above the EU threshold |

| Cost per dispute | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review committee | €1,100 | €1,300 | €2,000 |
| Buyer staff time **[A]** | €600 | €1,900 | €4,500 |
| Bidder legal cost **[A]** | €2,000 | €5,000 | €15,000 |
| **Per dispute** | **€3,700** | **€8,200** | **€21,500** |
| **Per year (~300 disputes)** | **€1.0M** | **€2.5M** | **€7.3M** |

**Speaker notes:** only ~7–8 disputes a year concern procurements below the simple threshold; half of challenged procurements are above the EU threshold. Full log transparency could *raise* challenges at first.

## 10. What the law lets an agent negotiate

**Headline:** real bargaining is only legal in small purchases and in procedures that announce negotiation in advance. Framework call-offs gain from competition and speed, not bargaining.

| Procedure | Negotiation allowed? | Rounds |
|---|---|---|
| Small purchase (< €50k / €100k) | ✅ Everything, within general principles and the buyer's internal rules | Unlimited |
| Simple procurement | ⚠️ Only if reserved in the documents, with all compliant tenderers equally | As announced |
| Competitive procedure with negotiation (below EU threshold) | ⚠️ Anything except minimum requirements and award criteria | Staged, then final sealed tenders |
| Framework call-off, no reopening | ❌ Contract follows framework terms | — |
| Framework mini-competition | ❌ One sealed round; e-auction allowed | 1 (+ e-auction) |
| Open / restricted (above EU threshold) | ❌ Strictly forbidden | — |

**Rules the negotiation envelope must encode**

1. Negotiable parameters are fixed in the published documents beforehand; an agent can't invent a lever at run time.
2. Minimum requirements and award criteria never move.
3. Any concession or clarification goes to every tenderer simultaneously.
4. Negotiation ends with a closed final round.
5. Every exchange is logged and reproducible.
6. The officer signs the award; nothing in the law permits an automated award.

**Speaker notes:** only an estimated ~20% of simple procurements reserve negotiation **[A]** (2–4% visible in notices, the rest unknown).

## 11. What comparable programmes achieved

**Headline:** speed gains are well evidenced. Price gains are small. Nobody has measured fewer disputes or more SMEs from automation.

| Programme | Measured effect | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| Prozorro Market e-catalogue (Ukraine) | 9 vs ~27 days (−58% to −67%) for standardised buys | B: like-for-like on the same platform |
| Prozorro core (Ukraine) | Prices 3.5–5.8% lower vs estimate; 5–6 days faster | B |
| KONEPS (Korea) | Bid review > 30 h → < 2 h | C |
| India & Indonesia e-procurement | No price effect; less delay; better quality | A: peer-reviewed causal |
| Walmart × Pactum agent negotiation | 1.5% savings (pilot), ~3% (scaled); 64–68% deal rate | C: secondary sources |
| World Bank systematic review | 5–10% for well-implemented reforms (ceiling) | A/B |

| Effect to borrow **[A]** | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycle time, below-threshold & call-offs | −25% | **−45%** | −60% |
| Buyer effort per procedure | −20% | **−35%** | −50% |
| Price effect of agent negotiation (addressable spend) | 0.5% | **1.5%** | 3.0% |
| Bids per procedure | +0% | **+10%** | +25% |
| Supplier SDK adoption by year 3 | 20% | **40%** | 65% |
| Evaluation disputes | 0% | **−10%** | −25% |

**Speaker notes**

- Excluded: a widely repeated "GSA pilot cut protests by 47%" claim with no GSA source behind it.
- Estonia went digital in 2018, so only digital-to-digital improvements transfer, not paper-to-digital headline savings (KONEPS USD 8bn, EC "5–20%").
- Walmart and Ukraine figures still need verifying against the original publications before the white paper.

**Headline:** the raw ranges per stream. They overlap and are **not additive**; the value model decides what counts.

| Stream | Basis | Indicative €/yr (low / base / high) |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer effort freed | Buyer effort −20 / −35 / −50% on ~€34M | €7M / **€12M** / €17M |
| Supplier response cost avoided | Supplier share of €111M; reduction not yet researched | open |
| Negotiated price gain | Agent negotiation 0.5 / 1.5 / 3% on negotiable below-EU spend (€0.64–1.26bn today, up to €1.1–2.2bn after reform) | ~€3M / **~€15–25M** / ~€65M |

| Dispute cost avoided | 0 / −10 / −25% of €1.0–7.3M | €0 / **€0.25M** / €1.8M |

**Speaker notes**

- Gains the 1 Nov 2026 reform delivers on its own must be subtracted.
- Indicative, rounded figures for orientation only.

## 14. What we still don't know

| Gap | Why it matters | How to close it |
|---|---|---|
| Below-threshold spend outside the register | Biggest swing factor for the 2% claim | Treasury / RTK e-invoice data |
| Officer clarification time | The case's core cost-of-inaction claim is unmeasured | Timesheet sample from buyers |
| SME response cost and reasons for not bidding | "SMEs drop out" is unproven in Estonia | Supplier survey |
| Local price-vs-bidders effect | All price effects are borrowed from DK, EU, US | Regression on register data |
| Share of simple procurements reserving negotiation | Caps the negotiated price gain | Sample of procurement documents |
| Post-reform cycle times | The new baseline | Ministry 2026/2027 statistics |
| Which total value is authoritative | €5.1bn vs "over €8bn" in the same report | Ask the Ministry of Finance |
